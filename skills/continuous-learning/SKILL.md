# Learn from completed work

1. Identify what a future reader needs to know or decide. From the authorized records, establish what was tried, under which conditions and what actually happened. Keep the locator for the passage, change or observation that establishes the outcome within its authorized scope. Treat an explanation as a hypothesis while other causes still fit.
2. State the narrowest useful lesson. Specify when it applies, what to expect or do, and where that advice stops. Compare it with current guidance and any relevant contrary case. A single direct observation can support a narrow fact. Several retellings of one event count as one observation. Keep a one-time permission or preference with its original task.
3. Decide what the experience adds. If current guidance already covers the finding, point to that passage. If sources disagree, check whether their dates, versions or conditions explain the difference. Narrow the finding to what the evidence supports. When no new lesson survives, say why and leave the knowledge unchanged. Name a missing observation only if it would settle the question.
4. Check what the intended audience may receive. When the finding and citation can be shared, give both. When only the conclusion is cleared, give the permitted wording and attribution. Keep restricted evidence and locators with their authorized readers. Withhold any finding whose disclosure to this audience is prohibited or unresolved.
5. For an authorized update, put the lesson and its limits in the existing entry. Put any permitted citation beside the lesson or in the entry's linked evidence field. For a proposal, return the wording and any permitted citation together in the reply. A change to standing policy, a new skill or publication requires an assignment covering that action.
