# Final reply templates

Choose by what the operator wants to learn or decide. A template gives the order of information. Write it in the operator's language and omit clauses that do not apply. The examples and their record names are fictional. Use only evidence you inspected for the actual task.

## Direct answer

For a fact, definition or explanation. Give the answer in one sentence when that settles the question.

**Template** — `[Answer]. [Reason or qualification if it changes the meaning]. [Source for the claim].`

**Example** — The west desk closes at 18:00 on Fridays (opening hours, West desk row).

## Work result

For an action or deliverable the operator asked you to complete. If it partly succeeded, name the part they can use and what remains open.

**Template** — `[What changed or was delivered, and where]. [What confirmed it]. [Outstanding part and impact, if any].`

**Example** — The corrected timetable is live on the lobby board. The 09:10 readback shows the new departure at 14:30 (lobby board history, entry 48). The platform display still shows 14:00 (platform snapshot, 09:11).

## Progress update

For a checkpoint during ongoing work. Give the change since the last update, what remains and any effect on the operator. If nothing has moved, give the latest verified state and time.

**Template** — `[New or current state, as of when]. [What remains and why it matters]. [Evidence].`

**Example** — Since noon, two more rooms have reopened. The east room is the only one still closed (13:05 inspection register, rooms 1–6).

## Findings

For a review, investigation or request to draw conclusions. Lead with the finding that most affects the reader. Tie each distinct finding to its evidence and consequence. A clean review gives the checked scope and result.

**Template** — `[Main finding and consequence]. [Evidence]. [Other material finding or limit of the conclusion, if any].`

**Example** — The sign directs visitors to a closed entrance. It lists Gate A (visitor sign, revision 3), and the closure notice covers Gate A through Friday (maintenance notice 18). Visitors should use Gate C, the entrance listed in that notice.

## Recommendation

For advice or a comparison. Compare the options in scope on the operator's criterion. Include a discarded option only if the operator asked about it or its exclusion changes the choice. For a comparison alone, give the decisive difference. Recommend an option when asked or when the criteria establish a clear preference.

**Template** — `[Preferred option or decisive difference]. [Criterion, evidence and tradeoff]. [Condition that would change the choice, if relevant].`

**Example** — Use the local printer for today's posters. The finished posters can be collected at 11:00 today for €36. The €24 mail-order prints arrive tomorrow (quotes L4 and M2).

## Plan

For a request about what to do next or how to reach an outcome. Give steps in dependency order and a way to tell they worked. If work has already begun, use the current state to identify the first unfinished step. Use future or imperative wording for proposed work.

**Template** — `[First action and its prerequisite, if any]. [Following actions in order]. [Observable success condition and its basis].`

**Example** — After the rehearsal ends, unlock the public entrance and scan a sample ticket. Open the doors once the scanner shows “admitted” (opening guide, entry check).

## Operator input

For a decision, permission or missing fact that only the operator can supply. Ask the smallest question that unblocks the work and give the consequences of available choices. Once the operator can answer, stop. If someone else owns the blocker, report it under progress or work result.

**Template** — `[Specific question]? [What depends on the answer]. [Consequences of the available choices and their source, when applicable].`

**Example** — May I book the 60-seat hall for €340, which is €40 above the approved €300 limit? The 40-seat room is available on the same date for €280 (event brief, budget and venue quote Q8).

For “what did you find and what should we do?”, give the finding, its source and the recommendation together. For “return only the room code”, return the verified code alone. Keep working notes in context. Create a file when the requested deliverable or an applicable project rule calls for one.
